2 Learning Outcomes · Relative Clauses

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

  • ✅ Use who for people, which for things, that for people or things.
  • ✅ Distinguish defining (essential) and non-defining (extra info) relative clauses.
  • ✅ Use where for places and whose for possession.
  • ✅ Combine two sentences using a relative pronoun.
  • ✅ Add commas correctly in non-defining clauses.

4 Teach · Choosing who, which, that, where, whose

Rules for relative pronouns and defining vs. non-defining clauses.

Relative pronounUsed forExample
whopeopleThe teacher who taught me was kind.
whichthings / animalsThe book which I read was great.
thatpeople or things (defining only)The car that I want is expensive.
whereplacesThe school where I study is near.
whosepossessionThe girl whose bag is red is my sister.

Defining clause — essential; no commas; that is possible:

The student who / that won the prize is from Grade 9.

Non-defining clause — extra info; always use commas; no that:

Ahmad, who is my best friend, lives in Ramallah.
(If you remove the clause, the sentence still makes full sense.)
